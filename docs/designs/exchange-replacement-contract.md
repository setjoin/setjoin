# Design: Exchange Replacement Contract

Generated by /office-hours on 2026-08-23
Branch: main
Repo: setjoin/setjoin
Status: APPROVED
Mode: Startup

## Problem Statement

Setjoin targets organizations replacing on-premises or hybrid Microsoft Exchange, including organizations required by regulation or procurement policy to adopt domestic infrastructure. The product should be able to receive these migrations without promising a literal reimplementation of every Exchange command, role, or legacy artifact.

The immediate product risk is not one missing calendar permission. Prospects currently lack enough evidence that an unreleased Setjoin deployment can be installed, migrated, operated, cut over, and rolled back safely. At the same time, leaving Exchange-specific workflows unclassified makes future sales depend on ad-hoc answers and creates a real chance of discovering blocking gaps after a project has started.

The design therefore establishes an **Exchange Replacement Contract**: a durable, evidence-backed mapping from observed Exchange capabilities to Setjoin-native behavior, automated migration transforms, documented replacement workflows, or explicit unsupported/blocking status.

## Demand Evidence

- The founder has contact with administrators across roughly 20–30 on-premises and hybrid Exchange environments.
- Many of those organizations have considered replacement; some are being required to move to domestic products.
- One or two organizations have concrete replacement activity and may migrate by the end of 2026.
- The known lost opportunities were driven primarily by Setjoin's unreleased/maturity posture, not by a confirmed single permission or calendar feature gap.
- No observed workflow study or quantified feature-frequency inventory has yet been completed. Feature priority remains a hypothesis until that evidence exists.

## Status Quo

- Target administrators continue running Exchange Server 2016/2019 or hybrid Exchange because it is proven, familiar, scriptable, and already encodes their organization's permission and resource-booking policies.
- Prospective replacement projects evaluate more than mail transport: they need credible migration, system integration, domestic-stack compatibility, operational continuity, and acceptance evidence.
- Setjoin already implements substantial equivalents: calendar ACLs, default calendar sharing, mailbox delegates, shared calendars, resource calendars, EWS/M365 migration connectors, and broad verification front doors.
- Setjoin does not yet provide one customer-facing artifact that explains how a source Exchange configuration maps into the target system or proves that nothing was silently dropped.

## Target User & Narrowest Wedge

**Primary user:** the Exchange administrator responsible for a 300–1,000-user on-premises Exchange Server 2016/2019 estate and accountable for completing a domestic replacement without losing mail, permissions, calendars, resource-booking behavior, or rollback safety.

**Narrowest valuable wedge:** a read-only assessment and acceptance workflow that inventories a real Exchange environment, classifies every discovered migration-relevant capability, identifies blockers before procurement or cutover, and links each claim to a Setjoin test or documented replacement procedure.

The first implementation wave should close only high-frequency blockers revealed by the assessment data. Current candidate gaps are:

1. Exchange `LimitedDetails` calendar visibility: availability plus subject and location.
2. Named Room List grouping for room discovery.
3. The common subset of resource-booking approval, conflict, booking-window, and working-hours policies.
4. Import and post-migration verification of calendar permissions, delegates, resource groups, and resource policies.

Arbitrary Exchange mail-folder ACL parity is excluded from the first wave unless at least two representative organizations actively rely on it or an identified procurement requirement makes it a blocker. Setjoin's label-based mail model should not inherit Exchange's folder authority model without demand evidence.

## Constraints

- Setjoin is not formally released; production flags remain off until separate rollout decisions and retained evidence justify enabling them.
- Business authority remains in PL/pgSQL reducers. Assessment and migration code may translate source configuration but may not become a competing authorization authority.
- The source estate may be Exchange Server 2016/2019 or hybrid. Exchange Online-only behavior is not automatically required for the first contract version.
- Customer configuration exports may contain personal or sensitive organizational data. Collection must be opt-in, minimized, sanitized, encrypted in transit and at rest, retention-bounded, and excluded from the repository.
- Compatibility claims must fail closed. Unknown or malformed source configuration is a reported blocker, never silently ignored or coerced.
- A documented Setjoin-native alternative is acceptable only when it preserves the user's business outcome and can be demonstrated during acceptance.
- The contract must stay versioned because both Exchange and Setjoin behavior evolve.

## Premises

1. The product goal is **Exchange replacement readiness**, not parameter-for-parameter replication.
2. Every observed source capability has orthogonal classifications: `outcome_mapping = native | transformed | alternative | none`, `assessment_state = observed | not_observed | incomplete | unknown`, and a reducer-resolved `claim_state = supported | provisional | experimental | unassessed | unsupported | excluded`, plus `phase_impacts = [{phase, impact, reason}]`. No UI or document combines those axes into a compatibility score.
3. Migration credibility—deployment, data fidelity, coexistence, incremental synchronization, cutover, rollback, monitoring, and acceptance evidence—has higher near-term priority than adding speculative parity features.
4. Calendar and mailbox permission gaps are implemented in frequency-and-criticality order derived from real estates, not from the size of Microsoft's PowerShell surface.
5. No source configuration may disappear silently. Preserving an explicit warning is better than pretending compatibility.

## V1 Boundary and Definitions

### Included in v1

- Exchange Server 2016 and 2019 on-premises configuration discovery for identity prerequisites, mailbox/calendar delegation, calendar visibility, rooms/equipment, Room Lists, resource booking, and migration-critical topology.
- Hybrid estates only through an explicit topology adapter: the on-premises component is assessed fully within the domains above; the cloud component is either collected by a separately consented Exchange Online adapter or reported `collection_incomplete`. No cloud-side behavior is implied by an on-premises result.
- Exchange-specific deltas for coexistence, cutover, reconciliation, and rollback. General deployment, upgrade, load, domestic-stack certification, and continuity evidence remains owned by existing Setjoin verification programs and is referenced by immutable case ID.

### Excluded from v1 unless a named procurement blocker is produced

- PowerShell command compatibility as a public administration surface.
- Arbitrary mail-folder ACL semantic parity.
- Transport rules, retention, journaling, client compatibility, and the full domestic OS/database/cryptography/certification matrix. The collector records these domains as declared exclusions rather than implying coverage.
- Bidirectional coexistence writes and lossless rollback after unrestricted target-side mutation.

### Auditable terms

- **Representative estate:** one whose Exchange version/topology and 300–1,000-user operating profile match the target segment, with selection rationale recorded.
- **Discovered fact:** one schema-valid fact emitted by a named discovery-catalog version; inaccessible and excluded domains are completeness metadata, not facts silently omitted from the denominator.
- **Support cell:** a canonically serialized and digested tuple `(Exchange product family, named CU, exact build/security-update allowlist, topology, directory authority, PowerShell/runtime, cloud-adapter version if any, collector version, discovery-catalog/schema version, registry version, Setjoin release)`. An unknown build is `unsupported_source` until admitted by evidence. Behavior-bearing fields are every tuple field except the finite CU/build members explicitly named by an approved equivalence predicate; all other fields must match exactly. A Setjoin release change requires a rerun or a code-equivalence receipt tied to the changed files and cases.
- **Expected probe instance:** one applicable discovery-catalog probe multiplied by one root object found by a complete enumeration. Every expected instance must produce exactly one terminal result row; zero emitted facts cannot satisfy completeness.
- **Sanitized artifact:** an export that passes the versioned redaction profile, contains no message content by default, and has an approved retention owner and deletion date.
- **Correct workflow:** the named actor reaches the specified result, preserves stated security properties, and produces the expected audit/error evidence without developer assistance.
- **Zero unexplained loss:** every source entity in the reconciliation population resolves to matched, expected transformation, approved exclusion, or blocking discrepancy; the unexplained bucket is empty.

## Approaches Considered

### Approach A: Assessment Tool and Minimal Patches

Fastest route to customer evidence, but by itself would leave compatibility decisions fragmented and would not create a durable product contract.

### Approach B: Exchange Replacement Contract — Selected

Build a versioned capability registry, assessment report, migration mapping, evidence links, and a bounded first parity wave. This directly addresses migration trust while creating a disciplined path toward broad coverage.

### Approach C: Full Exchange Semantic Clone

Rejected because arbitrary folder ACL roles and the full `CalendarProcessing` surface would create an effectively unbounded pre-release scope, conflict with Setjoin's label model, and fail to address the maturity concern that caused known losses.

## Recommended Approach

### 1. Versioned discovery catalog and capability registry

The **discovery catalog** defines the audit denominator: support cells, required root enumerators, collection modules, source object types, properties, cmdlets, permissions, applicability rules, and explicit exclusions. Every required root enumerator emits its own terminal result independent of child rows. `complete_zero` is valid only with an independent inventory cross-check or signed customer zero-population attestation. A claim cannot be promoted when all applicable collected instances are zero/`not_applicable` unless the claim is specifically verified absence. Root enumeration must complete before child completeness is calculated. Each applicable probe × enumerated root object creates one expected probe instance and exactly one result state: `collected`, `unknown_value`, `permission_denied`, `collection_failed`, `unsupported_source`, or `not_applicable`. Only `collected` and `not_applicable` are complete; every other state is visible and fails the affected readiness claim closed. Reports publish root-enumerator, expected, emitted, missing, incomplete, excluded, and complete counts separately.

Maintain one machine-readable registry entry per migration-relevant Exchange capability. Each entry contains:

- stable capability ID and source Exchange versions;
- source object/cmdlet/property and discovery method;
- observed organization count and last-observed date;
- business outcome and criticality (`blocker`, `degraded`, `cosmetic`);
- Setjoin status (`native`, `transform`, `replacement`, `unsupported`);
- target reducer/API/UI or replacement runbook;
- migration and rollback behavior;
- security/privacy implications;
- exact automated and retained-evidence case IDs;
- owner, feature flag, and release state.

A registry entry represents a business capability or workflow, not an arbitrary cmdlet parameter. One externally stable capability ID owns zero or more separately identified implementation-unit IDs; E3 and E4 units have their own accountable owners, while the parent capability enters V1B only when every applicable unit passes. The wave cap counts parent capability IDs, not implementation units. Source properties are child mappings with applicability predicates. Schema versions are append-only within a major version; migrations preserve unknown fields. Waivers carry an owner, reason, expiry date, and review date but can never create or preserve a `supported` claim. Only derived, evidence-backed publishable claims may appear externally.

Claim state is computed from evidence records containing artifact IDs/digests, support-cell digest, capture timestamp, approvals, expiry timestamp, and invalidation inputs. Expiry or a behavior-bearing tuple change immediately makes the claim non-publishable until required evidence is restored; state is never maintained by manual assertion.

For a `replacement` entry, the contract records actor, trigger, preconditions, expected result, preserved security properties, added operational cost, known degradation, customer acceptance owner, and an executable demonstration case.

The registry is the authority for capability definitions and maximum claim policy;
reducers derive immutable evidence-bound claim snapshots. Their canonical join is the
only source for the unified management UI, assessment output, complete internal
feature inventory, Setjoin-versus-Exchange documentation, workbooks, JSON and
compatibility test selection. Handwritten feature tables may project it but may not
diverge from it or silently omit unassessed/excluded rows.

### 2. Read-only Exchange assessment

Provide modular least-privilege, read-only collectors for supported topology/version cells. Each module declares Exchange RBAC roles, PowerShell/runtime prerequisites, endpoints, timeout, retry policy, and partial-result semantics. A preflight proves reachability and permission coverage before assessment. The collector exports configuration facts rather than message content unless a separately authorized migration rehearsal requires content samples.

Initial discovery domains:

- mailbox types, delegates, Send As, Send on Behalf, and mailbox-folder ACLs;
- calendar folder permissions, defaults, delegates, and private-item behavior;
- room/equipment mailboxes, Room Lists, delegates, and calendar-processing policy;
- identity prerequisites, aliases, groups used by in-scope permissions, and migration-critical topology;
- estate size, database sizes, growth, coexistence constraints, and cutover dependencies needed to bound the migration.

Collector executables and schemas require release signatures. Every generated assessment requires a digest; deployments requiring chain of custody also require an operator/collector signature. Output is schema-versioned, redactable, and locally inspectable before transfer. Unknown values and incomplete domains retain their distinct result states and make the corresponding readiness claim incomplete.

### 3. Identity mapping contract

Permission and resource mappings use `(source_authority_fingerprint, object_type, native_anchor)` as the source key and explicit target IDs as target keys. The fingerprint is a keyed, versioned normalization owned by the customer deployment and remains linkable only inside that authority boundary. Anchor precedence is object-specific: directory object GUID plus forest/tenant authority for users and groups, Exchange mailbox/database GUID plus directory correlation for mailboxes and resources, and source-native external identity for external principals. One-to-one mapping is the default. Merge/split exceptions require an approved mapping record; collisions, delete/recreate detection, cross-forest moves, hybrid dual-object correlation, and tombstones are explicit result types. Matching may use approved aliases or directory anchors, but never email address alone. The assessment reports disabled, deleted, duplicate, external, nested-group, dynamic-group, and unresolved principals separately. Group expansion records source watermark, snapshot time, and provenance. An unresolved principal blocks only the phase impacts that depend on it; it can never be silently discarded. Every identity mapping and tombstone has a deletion or irreversible-anonymization date equal to the later of the named project-retention and rollback deadlines; the terminal action propagates through caches/backups and produces a verification receipt.

### 4. Customer-facing compatibility report

Generate two views from the same registry and collected facts:

- **Executive readiness:** blocker count, degraded workflows, estimated remediation waves, evidence status, and go/no-go recommendation.
- **Administrator detail:** each discovered source object, its target mapping, transformation, replacement steps, unsupported reason, and verification case.

The report must distinguish four mapping states visually and semantically and render every phase impact separately. “Replacement” must explain the new workflow; it may not be presented as native parity. “Unsupported” may block one phase while remaining cosmetic in another.

### 5. First-wave selection rule

The candidate backlog is not a selected wave. Before scoring, one parent capability must have a stable ID, applicable E3/E4 implementation-unit IDs and owners, dependencies, bounded acceptance case, exact source-property set, explicit exclusions, and an estimate that fits the fixed wave budget; oversized candidates are split or rejected. Eligible candidates receive `priority = frequency_points × impact_points`, where frequency is 1/2/3 for one, two, or all three assessed estates and impact is 1/3/5 for cosmetic, degraded, or blocker. A documented procurement/acceptance requirement sets impact to 5. The Wave Selection Gate chooses no more than four parent capability IDs by descending score; ties favor cutover impact, then lower implementation risk. The normal gate requires three-estate scoring and evidence from at least two estates. If only two estates are accessible, both must agree on use and impact and the product owner must label the evidence reduced; a documented procurement blocker may enter from one estate. No E3/E4 work starts before the gate and the corresponding E1 feasibility addendum passes.

Candidate backlog, not yet selected:

- add a calendar visibility level equivalent to `LimitedDetails` without weakening private/confidential-event redaction;
- add named Room List grouping over existing room/equipment resources;
- model a bounded, composable resource-policy subset rather than dozens of unrelated booleans;
- import each selected capability through its separately identified E4 mapping;
- retain unsupported source properties as a cross-cutting report/audit rule, not a wave capability.

Any resource-policy candidate must enumerate exact Exchange properties, observed-estate count, Setjoin semantics, exclusions, acceptance cases, and fixed implementation budget before admission.

Existing Setjoin behavior remains the preferred target when it preserves the business outcome. A compatibility facade may translate Exchange terminology in migration/admin UI without changing the internal authority model.

### 6. Migration-state, reconciliation, and acceptance contract

V1 separates business phase from run status. `migration_phase = assessment | initial_copy | shadow | cutover`; `run_status = active | paused | failed | rollback_in_progress | rolled_back | forward_recovery`. `rolled_back` and `forward_recovery` are terminal for the run. Retry from `paused` or retryable `failed` reuses the same idempotency keys and checkpoint; a non-retryable failure requires a new run.

| State | Authority and writes | Deletes/conflicts | Checkpoint and reversal |
|---|---|---|---|
| Assessment | Exchange authoritative; Setjoin receives no production writes | Not applicable | Re-run from immutable assessment artifact |
| Initial copy | Exchange authoritative; target user writes disabled | Source deletes are replayed or tombstoned; duplicate identities block | Restart/idempotently resume from per-entity cursor |
| Source-authoritative shadow | Exchange remains authoritative; Setjoin target writes are disabled except isolated acceptance accounts whose writes are journaled | Source wins; conflicting acceptance writes are reported, never auto-merged | Roll back to last signed source cursor; discard/replay journaled acceptance writes |
| Cutover | Explicit checkpoint transfers authority to Setjoin | Post-checkpoint source writes are unsupported and detected | Rollback is allowed only inside the agreed window and only while the target write journal remains reversible; otherwise forward recovery is required |

Authority/write policy comes from `migration_phase` even while `paused` or retryable `failed`; `rollback_in_progress` applies the cutover rollback fence, `rolled_back` restores Exchange authority, and `forward_recovery` keeps Setjoin authority under the incident plan.

RPO, RTO, shadow duration, and rollback window are per-project acceptance inputs and must be fixed before rehearsal. V1 makes no normal-user coexistence, bidirectional-write, or unlimited lossless-rollback claim. A write freeze is required during the rollback window unless every permitted target write has a tested inverse and journal replay case. Source and target checkpoints contain authority epoch, source watermark, target state, identity-map digest, reconciliation population, journal lower/upper sequence bounds, allowed write classes, and write-fence attestations. Detected post-checkpoint source writes block rollback unless explicitly reconciled. Each journaled target write is inverted, replayed, quarantined, or intentionally discarded under a recorded rule; rollback passes only when authority is restored, journals are fully dispositioned, entity counts reconcile, and the unexplained-loss bucket is empty.

Each state transition is durable and explicit:

| Transition | Authorizer | Entry guard/checkpoint | Exit evidence and failure path |
|---|---|---|---|
| Assessment → Initial copy | Migration lead + customer Exchange owner | signed assessment, identity mapping accepted, source watermark captured | complete initial reconciliation; otherwise `paused` or `failed` |
| Initial copy → Shadow | Migration lead | registry query shows zero non-complete expected instances with blocker impact for shadow, zero unresolved required identities, current required evidence tiers, passing reconciliation, valid checkpoint/cursor/journal digests | shadow delta reconciliation; timeout returns to `paused` |
| Shadow → Cutover | Customer acceptance owner + product release owner | same machine-evaluable guard for cutover plus signed cutover checkpoint, fixed RPO/RTO/rollback window, and valid write-fence attestation | authority-transfer receipt; failure enters `rollback_in_progress` or `forward_recovery` |
| Cutover → Rolled back | Migration lead under customer authorization | inside rollback window, write journal reversible, source checkpoint valid | replay/discard evidence plus source reconciliation |
| Cutover → Forward recovery | Customer owner + release owner | rollback guard failed or window expired | incident/recovery plan; no lossless-rollback claim |

Every transition receipt persists evaluated guard results plus registry, catalog, collector, support-cell and artifact digests. A non-complete blocker probe (`unknown_value`, `permission_denied`, `collection_failed`, `unsupported_source`, or missing) always fails the destination-phase guard.

Reconciliation is entity-specific, not a raw cross-system hash promise. Each entity contract defines source watermark/change token, snapshot time, quiescence assumption, mutation-during-enumeration handling, retry boundary, transition to incremental cursor, stable source key, normalized comparable fields, expected transformations, count partitions, sampling or full-population rule, tolerance, and approved exceptions. MIME, identifiers, folders-to-labels, and derived metadata use documented canonicalization rather than byte equality.

For every claimed capability, verification must cover discovery, mapping, authorization, migration, post-migration behavior, negative/error behavior, and rollback where applicable.

The project-level acceptance rehearsal must include:

1. source assessment against a representative sanitized Exchange estate;
2. initial migration and entity-specific canonical reconciliation;
3. bounded source-authoritative shadow synchronization;
4. permission and resource-policy verification using named administrator workflows;
5. cutover with explicit checkpoints;
6. rollback/rewind with authority restoration, complete journal disposition, passing entity-count reconciliation, and zero unexplained loss in the declared population;
7. retained machine-readable results and operator-readable report.

Evidence promotion is explicit:

| Tier / claim label | Eligible claims and required artifact | Review, freshness, invalidation |
|---|---|---|
| T0 fixture / `experimental` | deterministic schema, parser, report, and low-risk native mapping fixtures with digests and source fingerprint | engineering review; invalidated by relevant collector/catalog/schema/registry/Setjoin changes |
| T1 controlled live / `provisional` | controlled-lab Exchange support cell, retained commands/results, artifact digests, reconciliation, and negatives | migration + security review; rerun for changed support-cell tuple or after 180 days |
| T2 representative live / `supported` | customer-approved representative support cell, retained sanitized evidence, named acceptance cases, and release receipt | migration, security/privacy, customer, and release approval; rerun for changed behavior-bearing tuple or after 12 months |

A documented equivalence predicate may use one T2 cell to promote finite, named equivalent CU/build cells only when collector commands, schema, semantics and every non-build support-cell field match exactly and the release owner approves. Topology and major-version equivalence is never inferred. A cell without T1 remains `experimental` or `unassessed`; only a T1 cell may be `provisional`.

### 7. Data handling and decision ownership

- Collection processes locally by default under a named Exchange assessment operator. Upload/transfer is opt-in and uses approved authenticated encryption; restricted environments may use encrypted offline transfer with digest verification.
- The default redaction profile removes message content, credentials/tokens, free-text descriptions, and unnecessary personal attributes. Message samples require separate written authorization from the customer data owner.
- The data inventory covers raw exports, normalized facts, identity mappings, tombstones, reports, audit logs, backups, caches, test fixtures, and retained evidence. Every object declares allowed fields, storage, access roles, key owner, retention start, explicit deletion or irreversible-anonymization date, backup behavior, propagation method, verification receipt, and whether pseudonyms remain linkable. Raw exports default to deletion after report acceptance or 30 days, whichever is earlier; identity mappings/tombstones use the later of project and rollback deadlines; reports and retained evidence use separately approved project/release retention. Extensions require a named owner and date. Deletion propagates through caches/backups and produces a receipt. Collection, transfer, access, report generation, and deletion emit audit events.
- The migration lead owns technical evidence; security/privacy owners approve collection policy; the customer Exchange owner accepts replacements/degradations; the product release owner approves published claims and release; every waiver names an owner and expiry.

### 8. Product sequencing and ownership

Delivery is split so assessment value is not blocked by a full migration program:

- **V1A Assessment Contract = E0–E2.** Internal/pilot-only readiness report; it may label support-cell evidence but makes no replacement-ready claim.
- **V1B Replacement-Ready Contract = selected E3/E4 capability IDs plus E5.** Only V1B may publish a `supported` replacement claim.

| Milestone | Directly accountable role | Prerequisite | Exit artifact / verification | Go/no-go approver |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| E0 Discovery evidence + feasibility spike | Product discovery lead | administrator access and approved handling terms | observation record, estate inventories, seeded candidate backlog, source change-enumeration/cursor/delete/idempotency/journal/inverse feasibility results per in-scope entity | product owner |
| E1 Contract foundation | Compatibility contract owner | E0 findings | published v1 schemas, support-cell matrix, RBAC/runtime/endpoint/throttling matrix, preflight cases, privacy inventory, unknown preservation, T0 evidence | architecture + security/privacy owners |
| E2 Assessment wedge | Migration lead | E1 approved | signed collector/report package, root/probe completeness evidence, internal pilot report, no-mutating-command proof | product owner + security/privacy owner |
| Wave Selection Gate | Product owner | three-estate scoring or approved reduced-estate rule, procurement overrides, bounded estimates, E1 feasibility addenda | signed wave of no more than four parent capability IDs plus applicable E3/E4 unit IDs | product + architecture owners |
| E3 Target-behavior wave | Capability-unit owner | signed selected wave | reducer/API/UI behavior plus exact success/error/audit acceptance cases | architecture owner |
| E4 Mapping integration | Migration lead | corresponding E3 target behavior or accepted native target | idempotent mapping, source watermark/cursor, canonical reconciliation, unknown retention, T0/T1 evidence | migration + security owners |
| E5 Replacement-ready evidence | Release owner | selected E3/E4 IDs complete | required T2 support-cell evidence, signed cutover/rollback rehearsal, named workflow IDs and expected outcomes/errors/audits | customer acceptance owner + release owner |
| E6 Expansion | Product owner | post-release observations | next scored/sized capability wave | product owner |

E0/E1 must prove feasibility before any rollback claim: change enumeration, cursor durability, idempotency, delete semantics, snapshot consistency, journal completeness, and inverse operations for each selected entity. Each support cell publishes its exact RBAC, remoting/authentication, PowerShell/runtime, endpoint, throttling, and connectivity prerequisites plus a live/fixture proof that the collector invokes no mutating commands.

## Success Criteria

### V1A Assessment Contract

- The selected discovery-catalog version produces exactly one terminal row for every required root enumerator and every expected probe instance; zero rows are never treated as complete without the independent `complete_zero` evidence, zero instances are missing, and exclusions/non-complete states separately block affected claims.
- The collector/report package has T0 evidence, passes the support-cell preflight and no-mutating-command proof, and can produce one administrator-readable pilot report without developer intervention.
- Three representative estates are targeted for priority evidence; inability to access one does not block V1A, but a cell without T1 remains `experimental` or `unassessed`, while only a T1 cell may be `provisional`.
- Every candidate capability has a stable ID, phase impacts, evidence tier, source-property scope, and wave-eligibility result.

### V1B Replacement-Ready Contract

- Every externally `supported` claim has current, non-invalidated T2 evidence for its canonical support-cell digest or an approved finite CU/build equivalence predicate with all non-build fields identical; cells without T1 remain `experimental` or `unassessed` and cannot be published as supported.
- Every selected workflow ID names actor, expected result, exact error/audit outcomes, full-population or documented sample method, tolerance, and reconciliation population.
- One representative rehearsal proves assessment, initial copy, source-authoritative shadow, cutover, and checkpoint-bounded rollback with zero unexplained loss within the declared reconciliation population.
- Post-migration output enumerates every unsupported or degraded phase impact and the accountable acceptance decision.
- Arbitrary mail-folder ACL work remains deferred unless it passes the same evidence and wave-eligibility gates.
- A release decision can be made from retained evidence rather than founder or engineer assertion.

## Distribution Plan

The contract ships with Setjoin's existing on-premises distribution rather than as a separate SaaS authority:

- a version-matched, checksummed assessment collector package for the Exchange administrator;
- an admin-facing import/report workflow in the Setjoin deployment;
- a machine-readable assessment artifact suitable for offline transfer in restricted environments;
- operator documentation and acceptance templates versioned with each supported Setjoin release.

The existing build and release pipeline should produce and sign the collector executable, package, and schema together with the server release. Generated assessments carry digests and optional chain-of-custody signatures. Compatibility registry validation, discovery-catalog coverage, fixture-based assessment tests, and report schema checks run in CI. Live Exchange evidence remains an explicit version/topology release gate and is never replaced by fixtures.

## Dependencies

- Existing sharing/delegation authority and its calendar ACL, sharing-default, and mailbox-delegate reducers.
- Existing room/equipment resources, calendar booking reducer, Room Finder, and scheduling UI.
- Existing EWS and M365 migration connectors and migration orchestration.
- Existing deployment, transactional upgrade/rewind, verification catalog, and retained-evidence conventions.
- Access to representative Exchange Server 2016/2019 and hybrid estates under customer-approved data-handling terms.
- A separate implementation plan and architecture/security review before any collector, migration, reducer, or UI change.

## The Assignment

Within seven days, schedule one 60-minute screen-sharing session with the reachable Exchange administrator. Do not demonstrate Setjoin. Ask the administrator to perform, unassisted, these four tasks in their real environment:

1. audit and change another user's calendar visibility;
2. configure or troubleshoot a meeting room's booking policy;
3. grant and revoke mailbox/calendar delegation;
4. investigate one recent migration, permission, or calendar incident.

Record the exact steps, commands, objects touched, failure modes, frequency, and business consequence. With approval, collect a sanitized export of the relevant configuration. Produce a one-page frequency × criticality table; it seeds and ranks the candidate backlog but cannot select the wave until the Wave Selection Gate has the required estate evidence or procurement-blocker exception.
